## 3. Liability and regulation

### 3a. US: Regulation E (who eats the loss)

| # | Claim | Number | Source | URL | Date | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | Under Reg E (12 CFR 1005.2(m)), an "unauthorized" EFT is one started by someone other than the consumer without actual authority and from which the consumer gets no benefit. For those, consumer liability is capped (generally $50 if reported within 2 business days, $500 if within 60 days, per 12 CFR 1005.6) and **the bank absorbs the rest.** | $50 / $500 caps | CFPB Regulation E (12 CFR 1005) | https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1005/2/ | regulation in force | High (long-standing regulation text; liability tiers from 1005.6). |
| 3.2 | The CFPB's EFT FAQs say that if a fraudster **tricks a consumer into sharing account access info** and then makes the transfer, it is still "unauthorized," so the bank is liable. That is exactly the voice-clone account-takeover case. | n/a | CFPB Electronic Fund Transfers FAQs (copy hosted by Gfrlaw; summarized by NAFCU, Thompson Coburn) | https://www.consumerfinance.gov/compliance/compliance-resources/deposit-accounts-resources/electronic-fund-transfers/electronic-fund-transfers-faqs/ | 2021-06-04 (last update found) | Med/High (FAQ content consistent across 3 law-firm summaries; CFPB URL is the standard FAQ location, not opened this session). |
| 3.3 | **Authorized push-payment scams (the customer sends the money themselves, e.g. a coached senior) are generally NOT covered by Reg E.** Losses fall on the customer unless the bank or network chooses to reimburse. Zelle's network rule (2023) covers certain imposter scams, but Sen. Blumenthal's investigation found it affected less than 20% of scam victims. | <20% | Eco/consumer summaries citing Blumenthal investigation | https://www.bankingdive.com/news/warren-zelle-fraud-scams-report-truist-pnc-us-bank-america-jpmorgan-wells-fargo-aba-bpi-cba/633305/ | 2022–2024 | Med. Core legal point is High. |
| 3.4 | **Status of US scam-reimbursement proposals as of Oct 2026: none enacted.** The Protecting Consumers from Payment Scams Act (S.4943 / H.R.9303, Blumenthal/Warren/Waters, 2024) would have extended EFTA to fraudulently induced payments with a 50/50 split between sending and receiving banks; it did not pass. In March 2025 the CFPB dismissed, with prejudice, its Dec 2024 lawsuit against Zelle/Early Warning, JPMorgan, BofA and Wells Fargo (which alleged $870M in Zelle fraud losses). | $870M alleged | ABA Banking Journal (bill); American Banker / Payments Dive (dismissal) | https://bankingjournal.aba.com/2024/08/democrats-introduce-bill-to-require-reimbursements-for-electronic-transfer-fraud/ ; https://www.paymentsdive.com/news/cfpb-drops-fraud-suit-against-zelle-jpmorgan-wells-bank-of-america/741546/ | 2024-08; 2025-03 | Med.
